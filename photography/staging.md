# How real jewellery sits, and how each kind is photographed

Written 2026-10-14 after the owner's correction on ring C: the lying-flat photo showed the stone settings turned
sideways, with their holes visible, which no real ring can show; and three stones were drawn where the ring has five.
The owner: "do a research of how real rings are staying so you don't make mistakes like that … and do it for every
ring the right way no matter if it has stones or not … and this is not just for the rings, it is for everything."

Read this before writing any prompt, and check every result against it (`photography/workflow.md`, step 4).
Sources at the end; where the sources are thin, the rule is plain physics of how the piece is built.

## 1. Rules for every piece

1. **The piece is solid metal and real stones.** Nothing bends, floats, melts or rotates on its own. Every part keeps
   its real position relative to the rest of the piece.
2. **Stones sit in their settings and face outward from the metal they are set in.** A stone's flat top (the table)
   points away from the piece's surface, never sideways or inward. Turning the piece turns all its stones with it.
3. **What the back shows is what the maker left there:** the inside of a ring band shows only plain metal, a
   hallmark, and a small round hole behind each set stone (the open back of the setting). Never stones, never
   settings seen side-on.
4. **Count everything from the raw photos before the first prompt:** stones, beads/balls, links, prongs, sections.
   Write the numbers in the piece's sheet (`photography/pieces/`). If a count cannot be seen with certainty in the
   raw photos, **ask the owner before spending a credit** (owner, 2026-10-14).
5. **Two-tone metal is exact:** rhodium (white) is only where the atelier put it. Describe where it starts and stops
   (see ring C). Everything else stays the piece's gold.
6. **Gravity:** chains hang or lie in natural curves; pendants hang straight down from their bail; drop earrings
   hang vertically; a piece lying down touches the surface where it would really rest, with a contact shadow there.
7. **Scale:** on a hand, ear or neck the piece is its real size (a slim ring band is about 1.2–1.5 mm). Never
   enlarged for effect.
8. **Pairs match:** two earrings are mirror images, same size, same angle logic.
9. **One piece per photo** unless it is a set; no props touching the piece; no text.

## 2. Rings

Parts: **shank** (the band round the finger), **head** (where the main stone sits), **setting** (bezel = a metal rim
round the stone; prongs = small claws), **gallery** (the metal under the head). Stones are set in the outer surface of
the band and face outward.

| Shot | How the ring sits | What must be true |
|---|---|---|
| 01 standing (main) | Upright on the lower shank, three-quarter view, camera slightly above, so the front arc and the inside of the band read as an oval (ring A). | The decorated top faces the camera; the back of the band behind it shows only plain metal and the small holes. |
| 02 lying flat | Plain bands (ring A, B): flat, seen from above at about 45°. **Stone rings: this is the main photo, next row.** | A plain band's whole circle reads as an oval. |
| Stone rings: 01 lying (main) | **Flat on the surface, not tilted** (as moonmagic shows stone rings; owner, 2026-10-14 „A“), seen from the front and a little above (about 25–30°). The stone part of the band is at the front, its stones on the front edge facing the camera; the plain part at the back, higher in the picture, its inside visible. No standing photo for stone rings. | Stones face the camera, round, never sideways. From the inside only the small holes show. Stones as the sheet names them (zircons: clear and sparkling, never milky). |
| 03 on the hand | Ring finger, near its base, decorated part on top of the finger, real scale. | Stones face up/out from the finger; the part under the finger is the plain lower shank. |

- **Bands of beads or balls (ring A):** beads all round or as in the raw photos; count them roughly and keep their size.
- **Rings with a row of small stones (ring C):** count the stones and the beads between them; the stones face outward
  from the band along the top; the setting colour (rhodium rim) exactly as described in the piece's sheet.
- **Solitaire (one main stone, ring D):** the main stone's table faces the camera in the main photo, tilted toward
  the camera by about 10–20° for sparkle; prongs or bezel exactly as in the raw photos (count prongs). The shank
  narrows or stays even exactly as the raw photos show.

## 3. Earrings

| Kind | How it is shown |
|---|---|
| Studs | Face toward the camera, slightly tilted so the stones catch light; the post goes straight back, not visible from the front. Shown as a pair, side by side, or one on its back showing the post and butterfly. |
| Drops / dangles | Hanging straight down (gravity), seen from the front; a side view shows the length. |
| Hoops | Standing on their edge, front view, the opening/clasp at the bottom or behind. |
| On the ear | Real size; the drop hangs straight; studs sit flat on the lobe. |

## 4. Pendants and necklaces

- The pendant hangs from its **bail** (the loop at the top), straight down, front facing the camera.
- Lying down: the chain makes a soft natural curve (a circle or a gentle "U"), never kinked; the pendant lies flat,
  front up, at the bottom of the curve.
- Worn: on the neck at real size, the pendant at the natural drop point for the chain's length.
- Count chain type and length details from the raw photos; do not invent a different link.

## 5. Bracelets

- Bangles stand on edge (like a ring's main photo) or lie tilted with the decorated part facing the camera.
- Chain bracelets lie in a soft curve or a closed oval, clasp at the back or bottom.
- Worn: on the wrist at real size.

## 6. Sets

All pieces of the set in one photo, each obeying its own rules above, none touching; then each piece alone.

## 7. Checklist before the first prompt (copy into the piece's sheet)

- [ ] Every raw photo opened and examined zoomed in (not just one or two).
- [ ] Counts written: stones, beads/balls, prongs, links, sections.
- [ ] Two-tone areas described (where the white/rhodium starts and stops).
- [ ] Inside/back described (holes, hallmark, plain).
- [ ] Unclear points asked of the owner, answers written in the sheet.
- [ ] Shots chosen for the piece's kind (§2–§6), with the position for each.
